Why TIPA blocks Nashville from limiting this

The Tennessee Information Protection Act (eff. July 1, 2025) reads on its face as a consumer-privacy statute. Two of its provisions matter here:

§ 47-18-3208(a)(3) — the law-enforcement exemption. A data controller or processor (MNPD's drone vendor, Skydio) may disclose personal data to any law-enforcement agency whenever it “reasonably and in good faith” believes a law or rule may have been violated. No warrant. No oversight trigger. No Metro agency in the loop.

§ 47-18-3315 — preemption of conflicting provisions. Any local ordinance that conflicts with TIPA is preempted by state law.

Combined effect: a Metro ordinance trying to bar Skydio from handing MNPD drone footage to TBI, ICE, DEA, or any other agency would conflict with the state-level permission, and so would be preempted. MNPD’s pledge that it won’t share the footage does not bind the vendor — and the city is not legally able to make it.

Nashville's Drone Surveillance Footprint

This map plots publicly disclosed Metro Nashville Police Drone-as-First-Responder flight paths against the city's most sensitive sites — schools, childcare, playgrounds, places of worship.

Tap any location to see whether drone cameras passed overhead and how your council district compares to the county on race, income, poverty, and housing burden.

Explore by district: the districts inside the 2-mile flight radius are labeled with their member’s name and a running count of DFR flights that have crossed them. Tap any district number on the map (or “Explore this district” in the panel) to see that district’s demographics and how many drones have entered it.

Scrub through time: use the timeline at the bottom — drag the slider or hit play to replay the flights in order. Drones in the air grow their path live; completed flights fade back. Tap ALL to show every flight at once again.

Camera reality: at ~150 ft altitude, MPD drones can identify faces and license plates at roughly 1.2 cm per pixel.

Data sources — where every layer comes from
  • Drone flight paths — MNPD Drone-as-First-Responder flight logs, publicly disclosed; the GitHub snapshot is enriched at load with the latest flights from the live Skydio ArcGIS feed. data ↗
  • Demographics & council-district breakdowns — U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey (ACS) 2017–2021 5-Year Estimates, compiled by Metropolitan Social Services, Know Your Community 2023. data ↗
  • Sensitive sites — schools, childcare, playgrounds & places of worship, compiled from Metro Nashville GIS & OpenStreetMap. data ↗
  • Council districts & precincts — Metro Nashville GIS; boundaries per BL2021-1052 (eff. Jan 2022). data ↗
  • Calls for service — MNPD “Calls for Service” (data.nashville.gov), since the May 26 pilot launch and filtered to points inside the 2-mile flight radius. data ↗
  • Incident reports — MNPD reported incidents (data.nashville.gov), same date and in-radius filters. data ↗
  • Census tracts (ACS) — U.S. Census Bureau ACS 5-Year Estimates by tract (Davidson County), pulled live from the Census API detailed tables (race/ethnicity from B03002); tract boundaries from Census TIGERweb. Shown for tracts intersecting the 2-mile radius. data ↗
  • Satellite imagery — Metro Nashville 2022 aerial imagery, with Esri World Imagery as fallback.
