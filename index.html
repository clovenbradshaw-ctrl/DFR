Why TIPA blocks Nashville from limiting this

The Tennessee Information Protection Act (eff. July 1, 2025) reads on its face as a consumer-privacy statute. Two of its provisions matter here:

§ 47-18-3208(a)(3) — the law-enforcement exemption. A data controller or processor (MNPD's drone vendor, Skydio) may disclose personal data to any law-enforcement agency whenever it “reasonably and in good faith” believes a law or rule may have been violated. No warrant. No oversight trigger. No Metro agency in the loop.

§ 47-18-3315 — preemption of conflicting provisions. Any local ordinance that conflicts with TIPA is preempted by state law.

Combined effect: a Metro ordinance trying to bar Skydio from handing MNPD drone footage to TBI, ICE, DEA, or any other agency would conflict with the state-level permission, and so would be preempted. MNPD’s pledge that it won’t share the footage does not bind the vendor — and the city is not legally able to make it.
